# 02 — Product Strategy

## 1. Strategic objective

Validate whether solo creators will pay for an execution-focused content operating system delivered through Google Sheets and Google Calendar.

## 2. Validation hypothesis

Creators will pay a one-time fee for a tool that:

- creates a realistic content plan;
- converts content into actionable tasks;
- pushes tasks into Google Calendar;
- tracks completion;
- recovers missed tasks;
- supports optional AI recommendations using the customer’s API key.

## 3. Value proposition

### Primary statement

CreatorOS converts content goals into a realistic weekly execution plan and keeps the creator accountable until publication.

### Supporting statement

Plan content, schedule production, recover missed work, repurpose intelligently, and improve consistency from one operating system.

## 4. Market position

CreatorOS should not be positioned as:

- a printable planner;
- a social-media calendar;
- a generic productivity sheet;
- a Canva template;
- a direct social scheduler.

It should be positioned as:

- a creator operating system;
- a content execution system;
- an AI-ready creator chief of staff;
- a workflow and accountability engine.

## 5. Customer segment priority

### Segment A — YouTube-led solo creators

Highest priority.

Characteristics:

- one long-form video every one or two weeks;
- uses Shorts or Reels;
- may operate a newsletter;
- struggles with scripting, editing, and repurposing;
- understands Google tools;
- can benefit from structured production.

### Segment B — Consultants and experts

Second priority.

Characteristics:

- publishes on LinkedIn;
- uses newsletters, webinars, or lead magnets;
- values professional workflow;
- may pay more than beginner creators.

### Segment C — Small agencies

Deferred.

Reason: Requires multi-client, collaboration, permissions, and reporting features outside MVP scope.

## 6. Commercial model

### Basic edition

- Rule-based planning
- Content database
- Production workflow
- Dashboard
- Calendar views
- No AI integration

Suggested test price: **$19–$29**

### Pro edition

- Everything in Basic
- Google Calendar push
- Reminder automation
- Missed-task recovery
- Optional customer-provided AI integration
- Repurposing suggestions

Suggested test price: **$39–$59**

### Future Agency edition

Deferred until demand is validated.

## 7. Distribution strategy

Initial channels:

- Gumroad or equivalent downloadable-product marketplace
- Direct website
- LinkedIn content
- YouTube demonstrations
- Creator communities
- Affiliate partners
- Product Hunt only after product maturity

## 8. Product packaging

Each purchase should include:

- Google Sheets template
- Apps Script code embedded or installable
- Setup wizard
- PDF or web-based setup guide
- Short tutorial videos
- API-key setup guide
- Troubleshooting guide
- Version and changelog page
- Sample creator workspace

## 9. Competitive advantage

CreatorOS cannot win through visual design alone. Its advantage must come from workflow intelligence.

The defensible elements are:

- task-generation logic;
- capacity calculation;
- production templates;
- recovery logic;
- repurposing logic;
- structured data model;
- implementation quality;
- user onboarding;
- niche-specific editions.

## 10. Key risks

### Risk 1 — Product becomes too broad

Mitigation: limit v1 to solo creators and content execution.

### Risk 2 — Setup complexity

Mitigation: guided setup wizard, sample data, clear buttons, and videos.

### Risk 3 — API-key friction

Mitigation: AI remains optional; provide provider-specific guides.

### Risk 4 — Apps Script authorization concerns

Mitigation: disclose permissions clearly and request only required scopes.

### Risk 5 — Buyers expect direct publishing

Mitigation: state clearly that CreatorOS schedules work, not social posts.

### Risk 6 — Template copying

Mitigation: compete through updates, documentation, support, brand, and workflow depth.

### Risk 7 — Google platform limits

Mitigation: build within Apps Script quotas and document known limits.

## 11. Success metrics for validation

### Commercial

- 100 qualified product-page visits
- 5% or better purchase conversion
- 20 paid customers
- refund rate below 8%
- at least 5 customers actively using calendar push

### Product

- setup completion above 70%
- first weekly plan created within 30 minutes
- calendar integration completed by at least 40% of Pro buyers
- weekly active usage by at least 30% after four weeks
- task completion rate improves after two weeks of use

### Qualitative

- users state that CreatorOS reduces planning time;
- users state that they know what to do each day;
- users use the recovery workflow;
- users request improvements rather than abandoning setup.

## 12. Go/no-go criteria

Proceed toward a SaaS or advanced version only if:

- at least 20 paying users are obtained;
- at least 8 use the product for four consecutive weeks;
- at least 5 request automation, collaboration, or native-app features;
- customer interviews confirm willingness to pay recurring fees.

Do not build a SaaS based only on positive comments or free users.
