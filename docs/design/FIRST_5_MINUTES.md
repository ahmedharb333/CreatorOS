# CreatorOS — First Five Minutes

> The highest-leverage experience in the product: the first five minutes carry disproportionate
> commercial weight (one-time purchase — no trial period to prove value).
> Foundations: [SALES_PSYCHOLOGY.md](SALES_PSYCHOLOGY.md), [UX_VISION.md](UX_VISION.md).
> Status: **approved baseline.** (This supersedes the earlier onboarding layout.)

---

## The job

Convert a skeptical creator from *"another spreadsheet template"* to *"this was built for me — and I
can see it working."* It must answer exactly one question, **demonstrated visually, never explained:**

> **"Why is this better than using Google Sheets myself?"**

The answer is the flywheel.

## The flow

```
   Impress  →  Demonstrate  →  Activate  →  Personalize
```

No forced tour. No setup gate. No blank workspace. No AI dependency.

### Impress — the finished world
The sheet opens to a designed HOME (gridlines off, warm paper, wordmark) with **one obvious
invitation** ("See it come alive") and a quiet secondary ("Start from scratch"). Clicking loads
**Alex Rivera's fully-alive studio** — a believable version of the creator's own future working day.
The Coach narrates: *"This is Alex, a creator like you, mid-week. Here's their studio."*

### Demonstrate — the flywheel (the commercial centerpiece)
**Do not explain the workflow. Show it.** On screen, the creator watches:

```
   Idea → Content → Production Plan → Tasks → TODAY → Execution Score updates
```

The system feels alive. The creator understands: **"I don't manage this. CreatorOS manages this."**
This is the referral trigger ("watch this") and the single most persuasive moment in the product.

### Orientation — exactly one beat
One orientation beat only: the **Execution Score.** Its purpose is not education — it is **confidence**
("this one number is whether you did what you said you'd do").

### Activate — one tiny real action
*"Your turn — drop one idea. Just a title."* The first action takes **under 10 seconds**, and the
creator immediately sees their idea become part of the system. Confirmation is **evidence, not
celebration**: *"Captured. Your first idea is now part of your workflow."* (Confetti is reserved for
meaningful milestones only.)

### Ownership transfer — the commitment moment
A deliberate, important transition from the demo to their own studio:

> **Start My Studio**
> "We'll keep everything you've just learned. We'll remove the demo content. Your own workspace
> starts clean."

This is the moment the creator mentally commits.

### Personalize — later, optional
Only after value: *"Want CreatorOS tuned to your channel? 2 minutes — or keep exploring."* Setup is
deferred, progressive, smart-defaulted, escapable ("Finish later"), and rewarded (a Moment that flows
into building the first real week).

## Buyer's-remorse prevention

Immediately after purchase, the experience must provide evidence the decision was justified. The
creator must **never** be forced to configure the tool before seeing value. This is why the order is
sample → demonstrate → activate → personalize.

## AI

**Removed completely from the critical onboarding path.** The first impression must succeed with zero
AI configuration. AI appears only after the creator already understands the product.

## The sample workspace (a first-class product asset)

Treat it like a movie set. Every item — every task, content piece, metric, recommendation, and
recovery example — has a reason to exist and tells part of the story. It is not test data; it is part
of the product experience, and it is an **aspirational preview** of the creator's own future day. (It
depends on the sample being flawless — a first-five dependency; see
[IMPLEMENTATION_DEPENDENCIES.md](IMPLEMENTATION_DEPENDENCIES.md).)

## First-run edge cases

- **"Start from scratch" chosen first:** go to deferred setup, still offering "or see a sample first."
- **Tour skipped:** HOME is self-explanatory (verdict + one move); the Coach stays available.
- **Nothing added:** HOME's empty state keeps one gentle next action; no nagging.
- **Keeping vs starting clean:** the ownership transfer clearly separates the demo from real work.

## Success metric

Five minutes in, a cold creator has: (1) never once thought "spreadsheet"; (2) **seen the flywheel
run** before any setup; (3) completed one real action and been reassured with evidence; (4) thought
*"okay, this is actually for me."* That thought is the $29 decision being made.

## Portable Core (onboarding framework)

> Show the finished world → demonstrate the engine → let the user perform one tiny real action →
> transfer ownership → personalize later.

Reusable for every future toolkit; the domain layer swaps only the demonstrated mechanism and
vocabulary.
